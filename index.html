Before you begin

This guide is for identification interest only. Do not pick, touch, or eat any plant, berry, fungus, or other organism based solely on this guide. Many safe-looking species have dangerous lookalikes. Always wash hands after being outdoors. Remember: every plant and animal is part of a food web — even if you are confident in an ID, please look rather than pick. We take no responsibility for any harm arising from use of this guide.

⚠ View Key Dangers

Key dangers include

  • Giant Hogweed Dangerous — Sap causes severe burns in sunlight (phototoxic). Do not touch.
  • Hemlock (Conium maculatum) Dangerous — Extremely toxic. Resembles Cow Parsley. Purple-blotched stems, musty smell.
  • Arum Lily / Lords & Ladies Dangerous — Toxic berries and leaves cause burning, rash, and swelling.
  • Deadly Nightshade (Atropa belladonna) Dangerous — All parts highly toxic. Shiny black berries may attract children.
  • Stinging Nettle Caution — Stinging hairs cause temporary skin irritation. Dock leaves provide relief.

Our Locations

This guide focuses on wildlife spotted at two special local sites, plus South UK generally. Each has its own character and seasonal highlights.

Old Down

Basingstoke
24acres
12hachalk grassland
🦋priority site

Old Down is a remarkable 24-acre site of calcareous (chalk) grassland on the southern edge of Basingstoke. At its heart is a wildflower meadow rich with native grasses and wildflowers, surrounded by ancient hedgerows, copses, and tree verges that provide habitat corridors for wildlife.

The site is one of Hampshire's most important locations for the Small Blue butterfly, the county's smallest butterfly, recording some of the highest numbers in the county. Kidney Vetch, its sole food plant, grows here in abundance across the chalk slopes.

Spring brings carpets of Cowslips across the grassland, along with early-purple orchids and a succession of wildflowers through summer. Hedgerows of Hawthorn, Blackthorn, and Bramble provide nesting habitat for birds and food for autumn migrants.

Look out for

Small Blue Cowslip Chalkhill Blue Marbled White Common Spotted Orchid Kestrel Skylark

Best times to visit

Spring Cowslips, early butterflies, nesting birds: April to May
Summer Peak butterfly season, orchids, full meadow: June to August
Autumn Haws and sloes, migrant birds, fungi: September to October

Managed by volunteer work parties every Thursday (10am–12pm) and monthly weekends. All welcome.

Sholing Valley

Southampton
LNRnature reserve
1000+species recorded
🌿urban wildlife

Sholing Valley Local Nature Reserve is a hidden gem tucked within suburban Southampton, a mosaic of ancient woodland, acid grassland, wetland fen, and scrub that supports an astonishing diversity of wildlife for an urban site.

The valley's acidic soils support heathland plants and specialist invertebrates rarely found in the wider Southampton area. The stream corridor creates a wet habitat that attracts dragonflies, damselflies, and amphibians including the Common Frog.

Over 1,000 species have been recorded in the valley, including numerous invertebrate species not otherwise found in the city. The ancient woodland holds veteran trees supporting lichens, mosses, and hole-nesting birds.

Look out for

Common Frog Dragonflies Red Kite Peacock Butterfly Stinging Nettle Bramble Cow Parsley

Best times to visit

Spring Frog spawn, Cow Parsley, first butterflies: March to May
Summer Dragonflies, grassland flowers, invertebrates: June to August
Autumn Fungi, berry-laden scrub, fungi: September to November

Volunteer days are held twice a month (excluding August and December).

  • Last Sunday of each month, 10am–3pm. Lunch is provided.
  • Last Friday of each month, 10am–3pm. Please bring your own lunch.

South UK General

Hampshire & surrounds

Species marked as South UK General are widespread across Hampshire, Wiltshire, Dorset, and neighbouring counties. These are the common wildflowers, birds, and invertebrates you're likely to encounter on any walk in southern England, in hedgerows, gardens, woodland edges, and farmland.

If you spot something not recorded at Old Down or Sholing Valley, select South UK General when submitting a sighting suggestion.

About Old Down

A field guide to Old Down, Basingstoke

What is Old Down?

Old Down is 16 hectares of open chalk grassland, planted woodland and a narrow ancient woodland mainly of beech, together with Oak, Whitebeam, Holly, Sycamore and Yew. The open grassland was used to grow arable crops until around 2000 and is now being carefully managed to restore the diverse wildflowers associated with chalk soils. There are wonderful views from the highest point — also the location of an ancient burial ground — across the neighbouring countryside.

Why it's special

🦋

Priority site for the Small Blue Butterfly as identified by Butterfly Conservation — highest recorded numbers across all Hampshire sites

🌸

Many other butterfly species across meadow and woodland edge

🌿

Five species of orchid recorded on site

⭐

After more than 7 years of conservation work, areas of the site are of Local Nature Reserve quality

🐝

Managed to improve wildflower diversity, increase insect and bird species, and maintain chalk grassland habitat

The site today

Old Down is managed by the Old Down Group — a friendly team of volunteers dedicated to maintaining this 24-acre site of woodland and open grassland for both wildlife and local people. The site also has a children's playground. Look out for new additions coming soon including a running track, brass rubbing posts and climbing sculptures.

Get involved

Work parties run every Thursday 10am–12noon at Old Down, meeting at Old Down Hall. Monthly weekend work parties also run — check social media for dates.

New volunteers are always welcome. Free training is available through Basingstoke and Deane Council covering tool use, first aid and wildlife skills. No experience needed — just enthusiasm.
